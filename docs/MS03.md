# MS03 — Last Bead Standing: rough-cut review note

**Closed phase:** meaningful progress as the first social-native rule experiment,
but creatively rejected by owner decision. All outputs and code are frozen;
none is approved for publication. The account below is historical. Its rendering
commands do not authorize regeneration. See [the current register](EXPERIMENTS.md).

**Rule:** a moving cyan bead hits a stationary ivory bead. That bead turns cyan
and starts moving; a short impact note and ring mark the conversion. Cyan/cyan
and wall bounces do not create additional beads. The on-screen rule and persistent
state colors explain the premise when muted.

**Protagonist shift:** first follow the lone cyan mover to learn the consequence.
Then follow the growing population and choose an ivory survivor. As ivory becomes
scarce, attention transfers to stationary survivors among the moving cyan crowd.
The last survivor is the lower-right ivory bead at preview coordinates (252, 410).
It is not singled out at the beginning; a thin ring identifies it once it is alone.

| Time | Intended viewing function |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 s | See the rule and first collision; the first conversion is at 0.900 s. |
| 2–7 s | Learn by separated repetitions at 3.400 and 6.100 s; one mover becomes four. |
| 7–16 s | Compound repeated consequences: 25 more conversions, with increasingly frequent notes and more motion. There are 29 movers and 14 ivory beads left at 16 s. |
| 16–20.825 s | Track diminishing survivors; five ivory remain at 19 s and one remains at 20.825 s. |
| 20.825–23.167 s | Follow the lone stationary survivor through two distinct near misses, then the actual contact. |
| 23.167–24 s | Stronger related impact/chord, all-cyan release, then a clean reset at 23.900 s. The last three frames return to the opening state. |

The two deliberately readable near misses are:

- **21.400 s:** cyan bead 30 passes horizontally above the survivor, leaving a
  **4-pixel edge gap** at closest approach. A brief airy flyby and `MISS!` cue
  distinguish it from a successful conversion; the ivory count stays at one.
- **22.300 s:** cyan bead 36 passes upward to its left, leaving a **5-pixel edge
  gap**. The same missed-hit language repeats without a state change.

The final hit is at **23.166667 s**, between bead 29 and the survivor. Their
centers are 19 pixels apart, equal to the two 9.5-pixel radii. The final bead turns
cyan and moves; its sound uses the conversion timbre with a stronger chord.

Every conversion has a short pitched impact, cycling a small major arpeggio.
Increasing conversion density supplies the foreground rhythm. Quiet dry wall
ticks support activity; they are thinned during the final focus. Near misses use
noise-based whooshes rather than the successful-hit note. There is no ambient
music bed or global glow payoff.

This is **directed, deterministic game choreography**, not uncontrolled collision
physics. Seeded positions, authored contact times, guided approaches, waiting-bead
clearance, and two explicit passing routes make the beats reproducible. Ordinary
cyan motion has wall and cyan/cyan bounce response. Numerical checks reject
unmarked cyan/ivory core overlaps, and the conversion log records contact spacing.
Those checks cannot establish comprehension, anticipation, or replay value.
