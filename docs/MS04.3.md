# MS04.3 — Seeded automated gameplay and genuine-match selection

## Research question and status

Can deterministic offline policies generate complete legal matches, preserve
their true outcomes, and surface promising candidates through transparent
attention proxies without scripting a winner?

The simulator, search, complete logical logs, and first 12-candidate search are
technically complete and owner-approved. This milestone creates no
video or audio timing, and its scores do not establish spectator quality.

## Deterministic match generation

`inception.matches` runs the owner-approved MS04.2 rules until one of three honest
outcomes:

- `won`: the engine declared a sole player after a completely settled cascade;
- `cascade_draw`: the engine detected a cycle or reached its explosion ceiling,
  rolled back that attempted move, and declared an explicit draw;
- `turn_limit`: the offline search stopped an otherwise active match, preserving
  a blank winner and active engine state.

Each match records its seed, player/policy assignments, every selected move,
per-turn ownership and orb counts, eliminated players, cascade measurements,
the complete globally sequenced MS04.2 event log, and final sparse board. Replaying
the recorded moves reproduces the same events and final state.

There is no concurrent simulation, mutable global random state, model, LLM,
agent framework, network service, or authored result. A match uses one private
standard-library pseudorandom generator initialized from its recorded seed.

## Configurable policies

Two policy families are implemented:

- `random` chooses uniformly from the current legal row-major moves.
- `tactical` scores every legal cell using bounded local facts: charge ratio,
  immediate explosion, adjacent opponent capture, adjacent chain potential,
  estimated territory change, and possible elimination/victory. Equal-valued
  moves use the match seed for a reproducible tie-break.

The tactical evaluator never substitutes for the engine. It chooses a coordinate;
the complete authoritative MS04.2 cascade then resolves that move. This avoids
the earlier inefficient design of replaying a potentially 10,000-explosion
cascade for every possible cell before every actual turn.

All three competitors in the first production search use identical tactical
weights, so the configuration does not grant a named color a stronger policy:

| Proxy | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Immediate opponent capture | 3.0 |
| Immediate/adjacent chain explosion | 1.5 |
| Possible opponent elimination | 18.0 |
| Possible victory | 40.0 |
| Charge ratio | 1.0 |
| Estimated territory change | 1.25 |

## Match scoring and selection

Scoring happens after complete simulation and cannot change any move or result.
The capped components are deliberately readable engineering proxies:

- completion: +25 for a genuine win, −12 for a turn limit, −20 for a cascade draw;
- early identity: up to 10 from the fraction of competitors still represented
  after approximately two rounds;
- lead changes: 3 each, capped at 6;
- threatened-elimination episodes: 2 each, capped at 6;
- cascade: 1.2 per peak-turn explosion capped at 12, plus 1.5 per maximum depth
  capped at 6;
- comeback: 1.2 per winner cell of maximum earlier deficit, capped at 15;
- late uncertainty: up to 12 from the average top-two ownership margin in the
  final quarter;
- opponent captures: 0.4 each, capped at 20.

Selection first restricts eligibility to genuinely completed winners when any
exist, then chooses the highest score with lower seed as the deterministic tie
break. If no candidate finishes, the highest-scoring unfinished match may be
reported, but its missing winner and completion reason remain unchanged.

Lead changes, cascades, deficits, and close ownership do not prove allegiance,
anticipation, comprehension, satisfaction, or replay interest. Human review is
still required after presentation timing and rendering exist.

## Important finding: high cascade-draw rate

Nine of twelve matches reached the explicit 10,000-explosion ceiling around
turns 93–99. They correctly returned `cascade_step_limit`, no winner, and a
rolled-back final attempted move. This is evidence that the approved conservative
orb rules and current tactical play frequently enter very long non-settling
cascades on a saturated three-team board.

The selector can still provide genuine completed candidates, but a 75% draw rate
is not an acceptable hidden production assumption. Do not rename these draws as
wins or silently raise the ceiling. Before scaling automated generation, decide
whether to study a rule variation, a policy that avoids unstable saturation, a
turn/orb cap with an explicit outcome, or another documented termination rule.
MS04.2 remains unchanged until the owner authorizes such a rule revision.

## Output and reproduction

`outputs/ms04.3/ms04.3-search-v1.json` is the 12.5 MB search record. It includes
all 12 complete candidate logs, scores, final states, deterministic ranking,
selected match reference, configuration, and source hashes. It is an engineering
artifact, not a publishable video.

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m inception.ms04_3 \
  --config configs/ms04.3.toml \
  --output outputs/ms04.3/ms04.3-search-v1.json
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests/test_ms04_3.py
.venv/bin/python tools/verify_particle_freeze.py
```

The exporter refuses an existing output unless `--overwrite` is supplied. That
flag applies only to the MS04.3 search JSON. MS04.4 may time and render the
unaltered selected match, but neither rendering nor audio is added here.
