# Spectator-game research hypotheses

The owner accepted MS03B as the concept proof closing the particle phase.
The observed creative direction is competition rather than fictional particle
physics: allegiance, reversible control, threatened elimination, comeback, and
an unmistakable color winner. No new viewer study or quantitative audience result
is invented here. This owner decision supports the pivot without proving retention,
replay, sharing, or satisfaction across an audience.

The active hypothesis is that a rules-consistent spectator game can sustain
attention through the following experiences:

| Candidate experience | Question for normal-speed phone review |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate allegiance | Do viewers spontaneously pick a team/color? |
| Understandable competition | Can viewers infer the consequences of a move or cascade? |
| Changing control | Do they notice a genuine lead change without a score overlay? |
| Threatened elimination | Do they recognize when their chosen team is in danger? |
| Reversal and late uncertainty | Does a comeback change their expectation of the winner? |
| Completion | Is the winner obvious from board state and color? |
| Replay interest | Does someone want to inspect the turning point again? |

MS04.3 match scoring now uses capped engineering proxies for these properties,
not psychological measurements: early survival, lead changes, threatened-
elimination episodes, cascade strength, winner deficit overcome, late ownership
margin, captures, and genuine completion. A count of lead changes or high cascade
depth cannot establish comprehension or pleasure. Rules remain consistent even
when a match is less dramatic; selection never fabricates an outcome.

The first search selected seed 4304 from three genuine winners, but nine of twelve
candidates ended in explicit cascade-step-limit draws. That draw rate is a rules/
policy warning, not audience evidence. MS04.4 now gives that match a timed visual
and sound presentation, but psychological conclusions still require phone review.

The earlier TikTok prototype's small live sample reported 97 views, a 4.4-second
average watch time, 1.2% completion, and its largest loss at 0:01. MS04.4 treats
this as a practical opening constraint, not proof of why viewers left: the first
orb appears at 0.035 seconds and all three team identities are visible inside the
first second. The central unanswered question is whether a genuine 39.8-second
match earns continued attention after that faster hook. See [MS04.4](MS04.4.md).

MS05 asks whether related team sounds and escalating event cues make placements,
charges, captures, cascades, elimination, and resolution easier to distinguish.
Check on a phone at normal speed, including mono playback. Distinct spectra,
finite PCM, and no clipping are technical evidence only. Test perceived team
identity, readable rhythm, harshness, fatigue, and whether escalation is audible.

Record actual viewer observations, including failures, separately from the
synthetic fixture and automated checks. The MS05 slice is not a gameplay attention
test. Do not add narration, a music bed, or the planned spoken hook to compensate
for unclear event sounds. See [MS05](MS05.md).

The [particle hypothesis register](archive/PSYCHOLOGY-particle-phase.md) is a
historical record. Its assembly timing study is no longer an active next step.
