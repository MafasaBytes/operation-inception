# Vision

Operation Inception explores procedural motion that makes a short visual event
clear, intriguing, and satisfying. We want to develop an original, recognisable
visual identity through experiments, rather than copy a familiar viral format.

Two initial families guide the work:

1. **Chaos to Order:** an irregular system resolves into a precise, legible form.
2. **Difficult Problem Resolved:** an apparently blocked mechanical or physical
   process is solved through events the viewer can understand.

These families share anticipation, changing uncertainty, and completion as
candidate experiences. They require different simulation mechanisms. Experiment
001 addresses only the first family; a future mechanical experiment may justify
collision physics and constraints.

## What the first milestone establishes

A local, reproducible path from configurable seeded motion to a vertical MP4,
with measurements and automated checks. The first composition uses restrained
color, ample space, continuous curved motion, precise geometry, and a deliberate
pause after completion. These choices are candidates for a visual vocabulary.
We have not demonstrated that they are distinctive or preferred by viewers.

Engineering success means the video can be regenerated, the motion behaves as
specified, and the experiment can be changed without rewriting the renderer.
Research success requires evidence from viewers. A clean render alone does not
establish psychological effects or commercial potential.

## Constraints

Research questions should justify new controls and complexity. Prefer Python,
small modules, minimal dependencies, and motion quality over elaborate scenes.
Treat stylised choreography honestly. Keep physics and rendering separate,
record assumptions, and preserve reproducibility for comparisons.

No dashboard, cloud deployment, publishing scheduler, social platform integration,
AI agents, or generative models belong in this milestone. We can revisit a
constraint when an actual experiment demonstrates a need.

## Near-term direction

Review the baseline on a phone at normal playback speed. Compare two convergence
timings using the same seeds and visual settings, collecting clarity,
anticipation, and completion ratings separately. Use the result to decide whether
to refine motion, reduce path crossings, or test a different geometric family.
Avoid interpreting an engineering metric as audience preference.
