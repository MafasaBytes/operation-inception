# Psychological research notes

This is an operational hypothesis register, not a literature review or a claim
that the proposed effects are established. No viewer study has been run. The
terms below guide questions and experimental controls; they do not guarantee
attention, satisfaction, retention, or sharing.

| Candidate mechanism | Experiment 001 hypothesis | Possible observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perceptual fluency | Emerging symmetry becomes easier to parse | Viewers identify the final form and report increasing clarity |
| Curiosity and uncertainty | A disordered opening creates interest in what will emerge | Viewers describe a question about the outcome; stopping behaviour may change |
| Prediction and anticipation | Continuous paths and staggered arrival make the result progressively inferable | Viewers predict the form before completion and rate movement as understandable |
| Completion | A precise landing and final pause strengthen the sense of resolution | Completion ratings change when the hold duration changes |
| Sensory satisfaction | Smooth motion and restrained visual treatment are pleasant to watch | Separate motion-quality and satisfaction ratings improve |

These hypotheses can conflict. Too much predictability may reduce curiosity;
extended uncertainty may feel aimless. A long pause may clarify completion or
feel slow. Very accurate geometry might be appreciated, unnoticed, or unhelpful.
We need observations, including negative results, to distinguish these outcomes.

## First comparison

Use two convergence durations, for example 4.0 and 5.2 seconds, preserving the
initial field, target geometry, drift, curvature, stagger, palette, FPS, and
final hold. Use a small fixed seed set (0, 42, 1001, 2026) in both conditions.
The different total durations are part of this initial pacing comparison, so it
does not isolate convergence speed from total video length. A later study could
keep total duration fixed and explicitly test where to allocate time.

Counterbalance presentation order across viewers; randomise which version is
shown first for each seed. Ask for separate 1–7 ratings of movement clarity,
anticipation, and final resolution, plus a preference and a short explanation.
Record sample size, exposure order, device, playback conditions, and prior
familiarity. Use consented, minimal data collection; no tracking infrastructure
is implemented here. An early small study provides exploratory evidence, not a
general audience claim. Predefine a larger study and its analysis before making
stronger conclusions.

## Measurement boundaries

RMS target error, exact settlement, spacing, and finite-difference speed diagnose
the generated system. They do not quantify perceptual fluency, curiosity, or
satisfaction. Target error depends on the assigned particle identities and may
improve while the image remains hard to parse. A minimum spacing metric exposes
overlaps but does not establish whether viewers find them distracting.

Review both the video and measurements. Record surprising or negative feedback
alongside preferences. Do not optimise many visual variables on the same small
viewer sample and then describe the result as confirmed.
