# Experiment 001B — Causal Assembly with Final Lock

**Closed phase:** creatively rejected by owner decision. All artifacts and code
are frozen experimental infrastructure. The descriptions below are historical;
no existing output is approved for publication. See [the current register](EXPERIMENTS.md).

This is one creative treatment, not a pacing comparison or evidence of audience
satisfaction. The question is whether a visible neighbor rule and a withheld
last piece strengthen anticipation and completion relative to Experiment 001.

| Phase | Intended emotional function | Visible action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00–0.43 s | Hook: establish immediate intention | One bright cyan bead travels toward the center through a disordered, muted field. No fade or idle lead-in. |
| 0.43–0.90 s | Explain the organizing rule | The seed locks; short signals reach its six neighbors, which move and turn warm as they lock. |
| 0.90–3.60 s | Make progress countable and predictable | Groups of at most six advance through adjacent shells; the warm stable core grows while the frontier is cyan. |
| 3.60–4.45 s | Withhold closure and redirect attention | Ordinary beads finish by 4.20 s. A cyan slot outline marks the missing right tip; one isolated cyan bead remains outside it. |
| 4.45–5.15 s | Concentrate anticipation on one journey | Only the final bead moves. It curves toward the slot, overshoots by 0.01 canvas widths, and corrects over the final 0.14 s. |
| 5.15–5.75 s | Payoff: the final part visibly affects the whole | Its lock triggers an outward light wave and a 1.8% collective compression/release. All beads become warm ivory. |
| 5.75–6.20 s | Release: make completion unambiguous | Exact, unchanged geometry and appearance for about 0.45 s, then cut. |

The intended organizing rule is: a locked bead activates its neighbors; moving
cyan becomes settled warm ivory. The final delayed bead is the one exception to
the normal schedule. Short parent-to-child signals make activation visible;
they are not a force simulation. Each ordinary lock has a five-frame scale and
color response without continuing positional drift.

Composition uses a 91-site regular hexagon, centered at (0.445, 0.78) in
canvas-width units. Including bead radii, it spans about 66.5% of the frame width,
with about 80 preview pixels of right clearance and 255 pixels below. Settled
beads are approximately nine pixels across at 360×640. These margins are a
composition choice, not a guarantee for every platform overlay layout.

The delivered seed is 1001. Core path clearance is planned, rather than produced
by collision physics. The existing renderer adds a shaded edge and small bead
highlight; the constant background bloom is reduced to 8% of the baseline.
Event light is concentrated on activation, arrivals, and the final lock.

Deliverables are `outputs/experiment001b-preview.mp4`,
`outputs/experiment001b-final.mp4`, and `outputs/experiment001b-contact-sheet.png`.
Both videos are 6.2 seconds / 186 frames at 30 FPS. The sheet uses decoded preview
frames at 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.8, 4.7, and 5.5 seconds. CSV and JSON sidecars retain
the existing measurements and provenance format. The original Experiment 001
videos, metrics, manifest, and earlier review package remain untouched.

Review silently at normal speed: can a viewer identify the starting point and
neighbor rule, notice the missing slot before entry, track the final bead, and
distinguish its lock from the smaller arrivals? Lock counts, smoothness, and
absence of sampled core overlaps cannot answer those experiential questions.
The original review questions above are historical. The owner subsequently
rejected this revision; no further polishing is planned.
